**Short answer:** Malaria and typhoid both cause fever, headache and body aches, so you can't reliably tell them apart from symptoms alone. A malaria rapid test or microscopy gives an answer in minutes; typhoid needs a stool antigen test or culture. Test first, then treat.

"Ni malaria tu" ("it's just malaria") is one of the most common phrases in Kenyan homes. But fever has many causes, and treating the wrong one wastes money and lets the real illness get worse.

## How the symptoms compare

| | Malaria | Typhoid |
|---|---|---|
| **Cause** | Parasite spread by mosquito bites | *Salmonella Typhi* bacteria in contaminated food or water |
| **Fever** | Often comes in waves with chills and sweating | Rises gradually over several days, often high and constant |
| **Stomach** | Nausea and vomiting can occur | Abdominal pain, constipation or diarrhoea are common |
| **Onset** | Usually 7–30 days after a mosquito bite | Usually 6–30 days after exposure |
| **Other signs** | Headache, muscle aches, tiredness | Headache, weakness, loss of appetite, sometimes a faint rash |

## Why testing matters

- **Malaria testing is quick.** A malaria rapid diagnostic test (mRDT) or microscopy gives results within minutes.
- **The Widal test is unreliable on its own.** It's still common in Kenya but often shows "positive" in people who don't have typhoid. That leads to unnecessary antibiotics and antibiotic resistance.
- **Better typhoid tests exist.** Stool antigen tests and cultures are more accurate. Your clinician will choose the right one.
- **It might be neither.** Urinary tract infections, flu, COVID-19, brucellosis and other infections also cause fever.

## Malaria in Nakuru

Nakuru's highland areas have less malaria than lake and coastal regions, but cases still occur, especially after travel to Kisumu, western Kenya or the coast. If you have a fever within a few weeks of such a trip, tell your clinician.

## When to come in immediately

Seek care straight away, at any hour, if someone with fever:

- Is confused, very drowsy or has a convulsion (fit)
- Can't keep fluids down or is passing very little urine
- Has difficulty breathing
- Is pregnant, a baby or a young child
- Has had fever for more than two or three days

## Prevention

- **Malaria:** Sleep under a treated mosquito net every night. Clear standing water around the home.
- **Typhoid:** Drink boiled or treated water, wash hands with soap after the toilet and before eating, and eat food that's freshly cooked and hot.
