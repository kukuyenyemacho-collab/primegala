**Short answer:** After giving birth, go to a health facility immediately, at any hour, if you have **heavy bleeding, fever, a severe headache or blurred vision, a painful swollen leg, difficulty breathing or chest pain, smelly discharge, a wound that is red or opening, or thoughts of harming yourself or your baby.** In an emergency, call 999 or 112.

The first weeks with a new baby are busy and tiring, and it's tempting to put your own aches aside. Most discomfort is normal. These signs are not.

## Go immediately if you have

- **Heavy bleeding:** soaking a pad in an hour or less, or passing large clots.
- **Fever or shivering,** which can signal infection of the womb, a wound or the breast.
- **A severe headache, blurred vision, seeing spots or a fit (convulsion).** High blood pressure can develop for the first time after birth.
- **Pain, swelling or redness in one calf or leg,** which can be a blood clot.
- **Difficulty breathing or chest pain,** which can mean a clot has moved to the lungs.
- **Smelly vaginal discharge** or worsening pain in your lower belly.
- **A tear, stitches or caesarean wound** that is red, swollen, oozing pus or coming apart.
- **A breast that is red, hot and painful,** especially with fever.
- **Pain or difficulty passing urine,** or not passing urine at all.
- **Thoughts of harming yourself or your baby,** or feeling you cannot cope. This is a medical emergency, not a weakness.

## What to do

- Don't wait until morning or your next clinic date.
- Ask someone to bring you, or call 999 or 112.
- Bring your Mother & Child Health booklet and ID, and tell the nurse at the door you recently gave birth.

## Help her get there

Partners and family often notice first. If a new mother seems confused, very unwell or not herself, help her get care straight away.
