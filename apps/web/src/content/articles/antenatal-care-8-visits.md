**Short answer:** Start antenatal care (ANC) before 12 weeks of pregnancy and plan for at least **eight contacts** with a health worker: one in the first trimester, two in the second and five in the third. Each visit checks you and your baby, and together they prepare you for a safe birth.

For years, many mothers in Kenya were told four antenatal visits were enough. In 2016 the World Health Organization raised the recommendation to eight, and Kenya adopted it, because more contacts mean problems like high blood pressure, anaemia and poor growth are caught in time.

## The 8-contact schedule

| Contact | When | Main focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Up to 12 weeks | Confirm pregnancy and dates, first tests, start supplements |
| 2 | 20 weeks | Baby's growth, blood pressure, counselling |
| 3 | 26 weeks | Growth, anaemia check, danger signs |
| 4 | 30 weeks | Blood pressure, baby's position, birth plan |
| 5 | 34 weeks | Growth, blood pressure, preparing for labour |
| 6 | 36 weeks | Baby's position, final birth planning |
| 7 | 38 weeks | Readiness for labour |
| 8 | 40 weeks | Review if not yet delivered |

## What happens at your first visit

- **Your history:** previous pregnancies, illnesses and medicines
- **Measurements:** blood pressure, weight and height
- **Tests:** haemoglobin (to check for anaemia), blood group and Rhesus factor, urine test, and screening for HIV, syphilis and hepatitis B
- **Supplements:** iron and folic acid to prevent anaemia and protect your baby's development
- **Vaccination:** tetanus–diphtheria protection for you and your newborn
- **Your booklet:** everything is recorded in your Mother & Child Health booklet. Bring it to every visit.

## At every visit after that

We check your blood pressure, weight, urine and your baby's growth and heartbeat, and we answer your questions. As your due date approaches we focus on the birth plan.

## Make a birth plan early

Decide early:

- **Where** you'll deliver, and a back-up facility if complications arise
- **How** you'll get there, day or night, and who you'll call
- **Who** will accompany you
- **Money and SHA:** confirm your SHA registration well before your due date
- **Your bag:** see our [hospital bag checklist](/health-hub/hospital-bag-checklist)

## Know the danger signs

Come to a facility immediately if you have bleeding, severe headache, blurred vision, swelling of the face or hands, fever, severe abdominal pain, fluid leaking or reduced baby movements. Read more in [pregnancy danger signs](/health-hub/pregnancy-danger-signs).
