**Short answer:** An HIV test at Primegala is confidential, uses a finger-prick sample and usually gives results in about **20 minutes**. If you think you were exposed in the last **72 hours**, come in immediately for **PEP**, medicine that can prevent infection.

Walking in for an HIV test takes courage. Here's exactly what happens, so there are no surprises.

## What happens during the test

1. **A private conversation.** A trained counsellor explains the test, asks about recent risks and answers your questions.
2. **A finger-prick.** A few drops of blood are tested with a rapid test kit.
3. **Your result.** Usually in about 20 minutes. The counsellor explains what it means.
4. **Next steps.** Whatever the result, you leave with a plan.

## If your result is negative

- **Stay negative:** condoms, knowing your partner's status, and PrEP if you're at ongoing risk.
- **Recent exposure?** If the risk was very recent, your counsellor may recommend a repeat test after a few weeks.

## If your result is positive

A positive result today is very different from twenty years ago. With treatment (antiretroviral therapy, ART), people with HIV live long, healthy lives. When treatment keeps the virus undetectable, HIV is not passed on to sexual partners (**Undetectable = Untransmittable**). We'll link you to care the same day.

## PEP: emergency prevention after exposure

**Post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP)** is a 28-day course of medicine that can prevent HIV after a possible exposure, such as unprotected sex, a condom breaking, sexual assault or a needle-stick injury.

- Start **as soon as possible, within 72 hours**.
- **Don't wait for morning.** We're open 24 hours.
- If you've experienced sexual assault, we will treat you with dignity and help you access care and support.

## PrEP: ongoing prevention

**Pre-exposure prophylaxis (PrEP)** is medicine taken by HIV-negative people at ongoing risk, for example someone whose partner is HIV-positive. Taken as prescribed, it's highly effective. Ask us whether PrEP is right for you.

## Your privacy is protected

Your result is shared only with you. Health information is protected under the Health Act, 2017 and the Data Protection Act, 2019. Read our [Privacy Policy](/legal/privacy-policy).
