**Short answer:** Kenya offers short-acting methods (pills, the Depo injection, condoms) and long-acting reversible methods (implants lasting 3–5 years and the IUCD/coil lasting up to 10–12 years). Long-acting methods are over 99% effective, and fertility returns after removal. A private counselling session helps you choose.

Every week we meet women who've put off family planning because of something a neighbour said. "Implants make you infertile." "The coil moves around the body." Neither is true. Here are the facts.

## The methods at a glance

| Method | How long it works | How effective* | Fertility after stopping |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Implant** (small rod in the upper arm) | 3 or 5 years, depending on type | Over 99% | Returns quickly after removal |
| **Copper IUCD / coil** | Up to 10–12 years | Over 99% | Returns quickly after removal |
| **Injection (Depo)** | 3 months per injection | About 96% with typical use | Can take several months to return |
| **Combined pill** | Taken daily | About 93% with typical use | Returns quickly |
| **Progestin-only pill** | Taken daily at the same time | About 93% with typical use | Returns quickly |
| **Male or female condom** | Each use | About 87% male / 79% female with typical use | Immediate |

*Typical-use effectiveness: how well a method works in real life, including missed pills or late injections.

**Condoms are the only method that also protects against HIV and other sexually transmitted infections.**

## Emergency contraception

If you've had unprotected sex or a method failed, emergency contraceptive pills work best as soon as possible, ideally within 72 hours. A copper IUCD inserted within five days is the most effective emergency option.

## Common side effects

- **Implants and injections:** changes in menstrual bleeding (irregular, lighter or absent periods) are common and not harmful.
- **Copper IUCD:** periods may be heavier or more crampy for the first few months.
- **Pills:** mild nausea or spotting in the first months usually settles.

If side effects bother you, come back: switching methods is always an option.

## Choosing what's right for you

Think about:

- **How long** you want to delay or space pregnancies
- **Whether you can remember** a daily pill or a three-monthly injection
- **Your health:** some conditions make certain methods unsuitable. Your provider will check.
- **Breastfeeding:** progestin-only methods, implants and the IUCD are suitable.
