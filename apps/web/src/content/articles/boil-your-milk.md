**Short answer:** Raw milk straight from the cow, goat or camel can carry germs that cause brucellosis, the cattle form of TB, and stomach infections with fever. Bringing milk to a full, rolling boil kills them. Boil all raw milk before drinking it or using it for chai, mala or yoghurt.

## Why boil raw milk?

- **Healthy-looking animals can still pass germs into their milk.**
- **Milk can be contaminated** during milking, in dirty containers or on the way to your home.
- **Some people are at higher risk:** children, pregnant women, older people and anyone with a weakened immune system.

## How to boil milk properly

1. Pour the milk into a clean sufuria.
2. Heat it until it froths up and rises. Warm or steaming is not enough.
3. Lower the heat a little and stir so it keeps bubbling for a short while without boiling over.
4. Making chai? Make sure the milk reaches a full boil with the tea leaves, not just hot.

## Store it safely

- Cool boiled milk with the lid on, and keep it covered.
- Store it in a clean, covered container, in a fridge if you have one.
- Without a fridge, use boiled milk the same day, and boil it again if it has been standing for hours.
- Wash milk containers with soap and hot water, then rinse with boiling water.

## Making mala or yoghurt at home

- Start with milk you have boiled, then let it cool until it is lukewarm.
- Add a spoonful of mala or yoghurt that was made from boiled or pasteurised milk.
- Leave it to ferment in a clean, covered container in a warm place.
