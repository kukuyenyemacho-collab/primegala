**Short answer:** Go to a 24-hour clinic straight away, without waiting for morning, if someone has difficulty breathing, chest pain, heavy bleeding, a seizure, confusion, signs of dehydration or, in pregnancy, bleeding, severe headache or reduced baby movements. For life-threatening emergencies, call **999** or **112**.

It's 11 p.m. and a family member is unwell. Do you wait for morning or go now? These guidelines help you decide.

## Call 999 or 112 immediately for

- Unconsciousness or unresponsiveness
- Severe chest pain or pressure, especially spreading to the arm or jaw
- Sudden weakness or numbness on one side of the body, drooping face or slurred speech (possible stroke)
- Severe bleeding that won't stop with pressure
- Serious road accident injuries

## Come to a 24-hour clinic now if

**Adults**

- High fever with confusion, stiff neck or a rash that doesn't fade when pressed
- Difficulty breathing or an asthma attack not relieved by the inhaler
- Persistent vomiting or diarrhoea with very little urine
- Severe abdominal pain
- Deep cuts, burns or animal bites
- Very high blood pressure with a severe headache or blurred vision

**Children**

- Fast or difficult breathing, or the chest pulling in with each breath
- Unable to drink or breastfeed, or vomiting everything
- Convulsions (fits)
- Unusually sleepy or difficult to wake
- Sunken eyes, no tears or a dry mouth (dehydration)
- Babies under 2 months with any fever

**Pregnancy**

- Vaginal bleeding
- Severe headache, blurred vision or swelling of the face and hands
- Baby moving less than usual
- Waters breaking before labour
- Fever or severe abdominal pain

## It can usually wait for a day visit if

- Mild cold or cough without breathing difficulty
- Mild rash without fever
- Routine medication refills
- Minor aches that improve with rest

If you're unsure, it's always reasonable to come in. We'd rather see you early.

## Your right to emergency treatment

Under **Article 43(2) of the Constitution of Kenya**, no one may be denied emergency medical treatment. SHA's Emergency, Chronic and Critical Illness Fund (ECCIF) supports emergency care for registered members.
