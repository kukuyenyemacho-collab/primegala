**Short answer:** High blood pressure (hypertension) usually causes **no symptoms**, which is why it's called the silent killer. A reading of **140/90 mmHg or higher** on repeated checks usually means hypertension. It's controlled by taking medicine daily, cutting salt, staying active and checking regularly.

Many of the people we diagnose with high blood pressure came in for something else entirely: a cough, a malaria test, an antenatal visit. They felt fine. That's exactly why every adult should know their numbers.

## Understanding the numbers

| Reading (mmHg) | What it means |
|---|---|
| Below 120/80 | Normal |
| 120–139 / 80–89 | Raised: time for lifestyle changes |
| 140/90 or higher (on repeat checks) | High blood pressure |
| 180/110 or higher | Very high: needs urgent assessment |

## Why it matters

Over time, uncontrolled high blood pressure damages blood vessels and can lead to **stroke, heart attack, heart failure, kidney disease** and **vision loss**.

## Seek care immediately if you have

- Severe headache with blurred vision
- Chest pain or difficulty breathing
- Sudden weakness or numbness on one side, a drooping face or slurred speech. Call **999 or 112**.

## 7 practical ways to control it

1. **Cut the salt.** Aim for less than one teaspoon of salt a day *in total*. Taste food before adding salt, and go easy on stock cubes, *royco*, crisps and processed meats.
2. **Fill half your plate with vegetables.** *Sukuma wiki*, *managu*, *terere*, cabbage, carrots, beans and fruit are all excellent.
3. **Move for 30 minutes a day.** Brisk walking counts, and so do shamba work and cycling.
4. **Limit alcohol** and **stop smoking.**
5. **Maintain a healthy weight.** Even a few kilos lost lowers blood pressure.
6. **Take your medicine every day,** even when you feel well. Use a phone alarm.
7. **Check regularly.** Every visit to a facility is a chance to check.

## Pregnancy and blood pressure

High blood pressure in pregnancy can be dangerous for mother and baby. That's why it's checked at every antenatal visit.
