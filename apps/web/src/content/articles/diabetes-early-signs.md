**Short answer:** Early signs of diabetes include **frequent urination, constant thirst, unusual hunger, unexplained weight loss, tiredness, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds and tingling in the feet**. A simple blood sugar test confirms it. A fasting level of **7.0 mmol/L or higher** usually indicates diabetes.

Diabetes is becoming more common in Kenya, including in rural and peri-urban areas like Nakuru North. Many people live with it for years without knowing. The good news is that with early diagnosis, people with diabetes live full, active lives.

## 8 early warning signs

1. Passing urine often, especially at night
2. Feeling very thirsty
3. Feeling hungry even after eating
4. Losing weight without trying
5. Feeling tired all the time
6. Blurred vision
7. Cuts and wounds that heal slowly, or frequent infections
8. Tingling, numbness or burning in the hands or feet

## Who should be tested

Get your blood sugar checked if you:

- Are over 40
- Are overweight or carry weight around the waist
- Have a parent or sibling with diabetes
- Have high blood pressure
- Had diabetes during pregnancy, or a baby weighing over 4 kg

## Understanding the numbers

| Test | Normal | Diabetes (usually) |
|---|---|---|
| Fasting blood sugar | Below 6.1 mmol/L | 7.0 mmol/L or higher |
| Random blood sugar | — | 11.1 mmol/L or higher with symptoms |
| HbA1c | Below 5.7% | 6.5% or higher |

A diagnosis is confirmed by a clinician, usually with a repeat test.

## Living well with diabetes

- **Eat for balance:** more vegetables, beans and whole grains; smaller portions of *ugali*, rice and *chapati*; fewer sugary drinks and sodas.
- **Stay active:** aim for 30 minutes of activity most days.
- **Take medicine as prescribed** and never run out.
- **Check your feet daily** for cuts or sores.
- **Get regular reviews** for blood sugar, blood pressure, eyes and kidneys.

## Seek care urgently if

Someone with diabetes is confused, very drowsy, vomiting repeatedly, breathing fast, or sweating and shaky (possible low blood sugar). Give sugar or a sweet drink if they're conscious and able to swallow, then come in.
